━━━ 1\. PREGNANCY AND PRENATAL CARE ━━━
1\. What are the early signs of pregnancy?
Early pregnancy signs may include a missed period, nausea, breast tenderness, fatigue, frequent urination and mild cramping. Symptoms differ from person to person. A pregnancy test can help confirm pregnancy.
2\. How do I know how far along I am?
Pregnancy is usually calculated from the first day of your last menstrual period. Your healthcare provider can confirm your pregnancy dates using an ultrasound.
3\. When should I start antenatal care?
Start antenatal care as soon as you discover you are pregnant. Early check-ups help monitor your health and your baby's development.
4\. How often should I attend antenatal appointments?
Appointment schedules depend on your pregnancy and healthcare provider. Attend every appointment recommended by your clinic.
5\. When can I feel my baby moving?
Most pregnant women first feel their baby's movements between 16 and 24 weeks. First-time mothers may notice movements later.
6\. When can I find out my baby's sex?
The baby's sex may sometimes be identified during an ultrasound around 18–22 weeks, depending on the baby's position and visibility.
7\. What is an anatomy scan?
An anatomy scan is an ultrasound usually performed around 18–22 weeks. It checks the baby's physical development, organs, growth and placenta.
8\. Is morning sickness normal?
Yes. Nausea and vomiting are common, particularly during early pregnancy. Eating small, frequent meals and drinking enough fluids may help. Seek medical attention if you cannot keep fluids down.
9\. Why am I always tired during pregnancy?
Pregnancy causes hormonal and physical changes that can increase tiredness. Rest, balanced meals and adequate hydration may help. Severe or persistent fatigue should be discussed with your healthcare provider.
10\. Is cramping normal during pregnancy?
Mild cramping can occur as your body changes. However, severe or persistent pain, bleeding, dizziness or fainting requires urgent medical assessment.
11\. Why am I experiencing back pain during pregnancy?
Back pain is common during pregnancy because of hormonal changes, posture changes and increasing body weight. Gentle pregnancy-safe movement may help.
12\. Can I exercise while pregnant?
Many pregnant women can exercise safely. Walking, swimming and pregnancy-approved exercises are often suitable. Ask your healthcare provider before starting or continuing an exercise programme.
13\. Can I sleep on my stomach while pregnant?
Early in pregnancy, sleeping on your stomach is generally fine if comfortable. As pregnancy progresses, side sleeping is recommended.
14\. How much water should I drink during pregnancy?
Hydration needs vary, but pregnant women generally need more fluids. Drink regularly throughout the day and increase fluids when it is hot or you are physically active.
15\. Can I drink coffee while pregnant?
Most guidelines recommend limiting caffeine to no more than 200 mg daily during pregnancy. Remember to include caffeine from tea, chocolate and other drinks.
16\. Can I eat fish during pregnancy?
Yes. Fish provides important nutrients, but choose low-mercury varieties and eat properly cooked fish. Avoid high-mercury fish and raw seafood.
17\. Why am I constipated during pregnancy?
Hormonal changes, iron supplements and pressure from the growing uterus can contribute to constipation. Drink enough water, eat fibre-rich foods and remain active if medically permitted.
18\. Is vaginal discharge normal during pregnancy?
Increased clear or milky-white discharge without an unpleasant smell can be normal. Discharge accompanied by itching, pain, a foul smell or bleeding should be assessed.
19\. What are pregnancy warning signs?
Warning signs include vaginal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, severe headaches, blurred vision, difficulty breathing, fainting, fever, fluid leaking from the vagina or reduced baby movements after a movement pattern has been established. Seek medical attention promptly.
20\. Can stress affect my pregnancy?
Occasional stress is common. However, prolonged or severe stress can affect your wellbeing. Speak to a healthcare professional if you feel overwhelmed.
━━━ 2\. MENSTRUAL HEALTH ━━━
21\. What is a menstrual cycle?
A menstrual cycle is the monthly hormonal process that prepares the body for a possible pregnancy. It starts on the first day of your period and ends when your next period begins.
22\. How long should a menstrual cycle last?
A typical adult menstrual cycle lasts approximately 21–35 days, although individual patterns vary.
23\. How many days should my period last?
Menstrual bleeding commonly lasts between 2 and 7 days.
24\. Why is my period irregular?
Irregular periods can be caused by stress, hormonal changes, weight changes, pregnancy, PCOS, thyroid conditions and other factors.
25\. Why do I experience period cramps?
Period cramps occur when the uterus contracts to shed its lining. Prostaglandins, which are hormone-like substances, contribute to these contractions.
26\. How can I relieve period pain?
A warm compress, gentle movement, rest and appropriate pain relief may help. Consult a healthcare professional if the pain is severe or persistent.
27\. Why is my period blood brown?
Brown menstrual blood is usually older blood that has taken longer to leave the uterus. It is commonly seen at the beginning or end of a period.
28\. Why is my period heavier than usual?
Heavy bleeding can be associated with hormonal changes, fibroids, polyps and other conditions. Seek medical advice if you soak through a pad every hour for several hours, feel faint or experience unusual bleeding.
29\. What is PMS?
Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) refers to physical and emotional symptoms experienced before menstruation, such as bloating, breast tenderness, mood changes and headaches.
30\. What is PCOS?
Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal condition that can affect menstrual cycles, ovulation, skin and fertility.
31\. What is endometriosis?
Endometriosis is a condition in which tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus. It may cause severe period pain, pelvic pain and fertility difficulties.
32\. Can I get pregnant during my period?
Yes. Although pregnancy is less likely during menstruation, it is still possible, especially with shorter or irregular cycles.
33\. What is ovulation?
Ovulation is when an ovary releases an egg. It usually happens once during a menstrual cycle.
34\. What is a missed period?
A missed period can be caused by pregnancy, stress, hormonal changes, weight changes, PCOS or other health conditions. Consider taking a pregnancy test if pregnancy is possible.
35\. When should I worry about irregular periods?
Seek medical advice if your periods suddenly become irregular, stop for several months without an explanation, become unusually heavy or are accompanied by severe pain.
━━━ 3\. POSTPARTUM RECOVERY ━━━
36\. What does postpartum mean?
Postpartum refers to the period after childbirth, when the mother's body begins recovering from pregnancy and delivery.
37\. How long does postpartum recovery take?
Initial physical recovery often takes around six weeks, but complete recovery varies. Some physical and emotional changes may continue for several months or longer.
38\. What is lochia?
Lochia is vaginal bleeding and discharge after childbirth. It consists of blood, mucus and uterine tissue and gradually decreases over several weeks.
39\. Is postpartum bleeding normal?
Yes. Bleeding after childbirth is expected. However, sudden heavy bleeding, large clots, dizziness, fainting or a foul smell requires urgent medical assessment.
40\. When will my period return after childbirth?
This varies. If you are breastfeeding, your period may take longer to return. If you are not breastfeeding, it may return within several weeks.
41\. Can I get pregnant while breastfeeding?
Yes. Ovulation can happen before your first postpartum period. Breastfeeding is only a reliable contraceptive method under specific conditions.
42\. When can I start exercising after giving birth?
Recovery depends on the type of delivery and your health. Gentle movement may be appropriate early on, but obtain medical clearance before returning to strenuous exercise.
43\. What is postpartum depression?
Postpartum depression is a mental health condition that can cause persistent sadness, anxiety, hopelessness, loss of interest and difficulty bonding with your baby. It requires professional support.
44\. What is the baby blues?
Baby blues are temporary emotional changes after childbirth, often involving crying, mood swings and anxiety. They usually improve within two weeks.
45\. How do I know if I have postpartum depression?
If sadness, anxiety, hopelessness or other emotional symptoms persist beyond two weeks or interfere with daily life, contact a healthcare professional.
46\. When does breast milk come in?
Colostrum, the first milk, is produced during pregnancy and immediately after birth. Larger volumes of milk usually increase around 2–5 days after delivery.
47\. What is colostrum?
Colostrum is the thick, yellowish first breast milk. It is rich in nutrients and antibodies that support your newborn.
48\. How can I care for my C-section wound?
Keep the wound clean and dry according to your healthcare provider's instructions. Seek medical attention if you notice increasing redness, swelling, pus, fever or worsening pain.
49\. What is diastasis recti?
Diastasis recti is the separation of the abdominal muscles along the midline. It commonly occurs during pregnancy and can persist after childbirth.
50\. Can diastasis recti be treated?
Yes. Appropriate rehabilitation exercises, particularly those guided by a pelvic health physiotherapist, can improve abdominal strength and function.
━━━ 4\. COSMETIC GYNAECOLOGY ━━━
51\. What is cosmetic gynaecology?
Cosmetic gynaecology refers to procedures intended to change the appearance of the external or internal genital area. Some procedures are also marketed for functional concerns.
52\. What is labiaplasty?
Labiaplasty is a surgical procedure that changes the size or shape of the labia, usually the labia minora.
53\. Is labiaplasty safe?
Like all surgery, labiaplasty carries risks, including bleeding, infection, scarring, pain and changes in sensation. Consult a qualified medical professional before considering the procedure.
54\. What is vaginal rejuvenation?
Vaginal rejuvenation is a broad marketing term used for procedures intended to alter vaginal appearance or function. Treatments differ, and evidence of benefit and safety varies.
55\. Does the vagina become loose after childbirth?
Pregnancy and childbirth can affect pelvic floor muscles and vaginal tissues. Recovery varies, and pelvic floor rehabilitation may help with certain symptoms.
56\. What is vaginal tightening?
Vaginal tightening describes treatments intended to change vaginal tissue or pelvic floor function. Some advertised treatments have limited evidence and may carry risks.
57\. What is vaginoplasty?
Vaginoplasty is surgery that reconstructs or modifies the vaginal canal. The reason for surgery, expected outcomes and risks should be discussed with a specialist.
58\. What is perineoplasty?
Perineoplasty is a surgical procedure that repairs or modifies the perineum, the area between the vaginal opening and anus.
59\. Can I change the colour of my intimate area?
Genital skin naturally varies in colour and may become darker due to hormones, genetics and friction. Avoid harsh bleaching products. Consult a dermatologist or gynaecologist about concerns.
60\. Is it normal for my labia to look different from other women's?
Yes. Labia naturally differ in size, shape, colour and symmetry. There is no single normal appearance.
61\. What are pelvic floor exercises?
Pelvic floor exercises, also called Kegel exercises, strengthen the muscles supporting the bladder, uterus and bowel.
62\. Can cosmetic gynaecology improve sexual satisfaction?
Results vary, and cosmetic procedures do not guarantee improved sexual satisfaction. Pain, arousal and sexual difficulties may have several underlying causes that should be assessed first.
63\. Who should perform cosmetic gynaecological procedures?
Procedures should only be performed by appropriately qualified and registered healthcare professionals with relevant training and experience.
64\. What should I consider before undergoing cosmetic gynaecology?
Consider the medical necessity, potential benefits, risks, recovery time, alternatives, costs and qualifications of the practitioner. Seek an independent medical opinion if uncertain.
━━━ 5\. SEXUAL AND REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH ━━━
65\. What is contraception?
Contraception refers to methods used to prevent pregnancy. Options include condoms, contraceptive pills, injections, implants and intrauterine devices (IUDs).
66\. What is the most effective contraception?
Long-acting reversible methods, such as implants and IUDs, are among the most effective contraceptive options. The best method depends on your health and preferences.
67\. Do condoms prevent sexually transmitted infections?
Correct and consistent condom use significantly reduces the risk of many sexually transmitted infections (STIs), although it does not eliminate all risk.
68\. What are STIs?
Sexually transmitted infections are infections commonly spread through sexual contact. Examples include HIV, chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis and genital herpes.
69\. What are the symptoms of an STI?
Symptoms may include unusual discharge, painful urination, sores, itching, pelvic pain or bleeding. Some STIs have no symptoms, so testing is important.
70\. Can I have an STI without knowing?
Yes. Many STIs do not cause noticeable symptoms. Regular testing is important if you are sexually active.
71\. What is HIV?
HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system. With appropriate treatment, people living with HIV can live long and healthy lives.
72\. What is cervical cancer?
Cervical cancer develops in the cells of the cervix. Persistent infection with certain high-risk types of human papillomavirus (HPV) is the main cause.
73\. What is a Pap smear?
A Pap smear is a screening test that looks for abnormal cells in the cervix that could potentially develop into cervical cancer.
74\. What is HPV?
Human papillomavirus is a common group of viruses, some of which can cause genital warts and others of which can cause cervical and other cancers.
75\. Can I get pregnant immediately after stopping contraception?
Yes. Fertility can return quickly after stopping many contraceptive methods, although the timing depends on the method.
76\. What is infertility?
Infertility is generally defined as not achieving pregnancy after 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse, or after six months for women aged 35 or older. Earlier assessment may be appropriate in some circumstances.
77\. What is an ectopic pregnancy?
An ectopic pregnancy occurs when a fertilised egg implants outside the uterus, commonly in a fallopian tube. It is a medical emergency.
78\. Can sexual intercourse cause pregnancy?
Yes. Pregnancy can occur when sperm enters the vagina and fertilises an egg.
79\. Is painful sex normal?
Pain during sex should not be ignored. It may be associated with dryness, infections, pelvic floor problems, endometriosis or other conditions. Consult a healthcare professional if it persists.
━━━ 6\. GENERAL WOMEN'S HEALTH AND WELLNESS ━━━
80\. Why is it important to visit a gynaecologist?
Regular gynaecological care helps monitor reproductive health, identify potential concerns and provide appropriate screening and advice.
81\. How often should I visit a gynaecologist?
Visit according to your individual healthcare needs and recommended screening schedule. You should also seek care whenever you experience concerning symptoms.
82\. What is a yeast infection?
A vaginal yeast infection is usually caused by an overgrowth of Candida fungus. Symptoms can include itching, irritation and thick white discharge.
83\. What is bacterial vaginosis?
Bacterial vaginosis is an imbalance of bacteria in the vagina. It can cause thin discharge and a fishy smell, although some people have no symptoms.
84\. How do I maintain vaginal hygiene?
Wash the external genital area gently with water and mild, unscented products if needed. Avoid vaginal douching and scented intimate products.
85\. Should I wash inside my vagina?
No. The vagina cleans itself naturally. Only wash the external genital area.
86\. Why do I experience vaginal itching?
Vaginal itching can be caused by infections, irritation, allergies or skin conditions. Avoid self-diagnosing and seek medical advice if symptoms persist.
87\. Why do I have unusual vaginal discharge?
Discharge changes throughout the menstrual cycle. Unusual colour, smell, itching, pain or irritation may indicate an infection and should be assessed.
88\. What is a urinary tract infection (UTI)?
A UTI is an infection affecting part of the urinary system. Common symptoms include burning during urination, frequent urination and lower abdominal discomfort.
89\. How can I prevent UTIs?
Drink enough fluids, avoid holding urine for long periods and practise good personal hygiene. Urinating after sexual intercourse may also be helpful.
90\. What is anaemia?
Anaemia occurs when the blood does not have enough healthy red blood cells or haemoglobin to carry sufficient oxygen. Iron deficiency is a common cause.
91\. What are the symptoms of anaemia?
Symptoms may include tiredness, weakness, dizziness, headaches, pale skin, shortness of breath and a rapid heartbeat. Blood tests are needed for diagnosis.
92\. What causes iron deficiency?
Iron deficiency can result from heavy menstrual bleeding, inadequate dietary iron, pregnancy, poor absorption or blood loss.
93\. Why is folic acid important?
Folic acid helps the body produce healthy cells. Taking folic acid before conception and during early pregnancy helps reduce the risk of neural tube defects in babies.
94\. What is menopause?
Menopause is the natural stage when menstrual periods permanently stop, usually between ages 45 and 55.
95\. What are the symptoms of menopause?
Symptoms may include hot flushes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, sleep difficulties, mood changes and irregular periods during the transition.
96\. What is perimenopause?
Perimenopause is the transition leading up to menopause, during which hormone levels fluctuate and menstrual cycles may become irregular.
97\. Why do I experience mood swings?
Mood changes can be associated with hormonal fluctuations, stress, sleep difficulties and various health conditions. Persistent or severe changes deserve professional support.
98\. How much sleep should a woman get?
Most adults need approximately 7–9 hours of sleep each night. Individual sleep requirements may vary.
99\. Why is calcium important for women?
Calcium supports healthy bones and teeth, muscle function and nerve signalling. Good sources include dairy products, fortified foods and certain leafy vegetables.
100\. How can I maintain a healthy lifestyle?
Aim for balanced nutrition, regular physical activity, sufficient sleep, hydration, stress management and routine healthcare appointments.
━━━ 7\. COMMON SYMPTOMS AND HEALTH CONCERNS ━━━
101\. Why do I experience lower abdominal pain?
Lower abdominal pain can have many causes, including menstrual cramps, urinary infections, digestive issues and reproductive health conditions. Severe or persistent pain needs medical assessment.
102\. Why am I experiencing bloating?
Bloating can be associated with digestion, constipation, hormonal changes and certain foods. Persistent or severe bloating should be assessed.
103\. Why do my breasts feel tender?
Breast tenderness can occur due to hormonal changes during menstruation, pregnancy, breastfeeding or certain medications.
104\. Is a breast lump always cancer?
No. Many breast lumps are benign, but every new or changing breast lump should be examined by a healthcare professional.
105\. Why do I experience headaches?
Headaches may be associated with dehydration, stress, hormonal changes, lack of sleep or other conditions. Sudden severe headaches or headaches with neurological symptoms need urgent medical attention.
106\. Why am I always tired?
Fatigue may be related to poor sleep, stress, anaemia, hormonal changes or other health conditions. Persistent fatigue should be investigated.
107\. Why am I urinating frequently?
Frequent urination may be associated with pregnancy, increased fluid intake, urinary infections, diabetes or other conditions.
108\. Why do I feel dizzy?
Dizziness can be caused by dehydration, low blood pressure, anaemia, low blood sugar or other conditions. Recurrent dizziness, fainting or breathing difficulties requires medical assessment.
109\. What is pelvic inflammatory disease?
Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) is an infection of the female reproductive organs, often associated with untreated STIs. Symptoms can include pelvic pain, fever and unusual discharge.
110\. When should I seek emergency medical help?
Seek emergency care for severe abdominal or pelvic pain, heavy bleeding, fainting, severe breathing difficulties, chest pain, seizures or other sudden serious symptoms.
━━━ 8\. MEDICATION, SUPPLEMENTS AND NUTRITION ━━━
111\. Why are prenatal vitamins important?
Prenatal vitamins provide nutrients that support maternal health and fetal development. Your healthcare provider can recommend the appropriate supplements.
112\. Can I take iron supplements during pregnancy?
Iron supplements are commonly prescribed during pregnancy to prevent or treat iron deficiency. Take them according to your healthcare provider's instructions.
113\. Can I take iron and calcium together?
Calcium can interfere with iron absorption. If you take both supplements, ask your healthcare provider or pharmacist about spacing them apart.
114\. What foods are rich in iron?
Iron-rich foods include red meat, beans, lentils, spinach, fortified cereals and some seafood. Vitamin C can help the body absorb plant-based iron.
115\. Can I take medication while pregnant?
Not all medicines are safe during pregnancy. Always consult your healthcare provider or pharmacist before taking prescription, over-the-counter or herbal medication.
116\. Are herbal remedies safe during pregnancy?
Not necessarily. Some herbal products can affect pregnancy or interact with medication. Consult your healthcare provider before using them.
117\. What is a balanced diet?
A balanced diet includes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, protein sources, healthy fats and adequate fluids.
118\. Why is vitamin D important?
Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium and supports bone health. Your healthcare provider can advise whether supplementation is necessary.
119\. Can I skip meals during pregnancy?
Regular, nutritious meals help support your health and your baby's development. If you struggle to eat or experience persistent vomiting, speak to your healthcare provider.
120\. What should I avoid eating during pregnancy?
Avoid raw or undercooked animal products, unpasteurised dairy and high-mercury fish. Follow food-safety recommendations and ask your clinic about foods you are unsure of.

Her Health is a women's healthcare application designed to provide accessible health information and support users in monitoring different aspects of their health.
122\. What can I do on Her Health?
You can use Her Health to access women's health information, track relevant health activities, maintain a personal journal and access the available health-support features.
123\. Is Her Health a replacement for a doctor?
No. Her Health is an informational and tracking tool. It does not replace professional medical consultations, diagnosis or treatment.
124\. Can Her Health diagnose medical conditions?
No. Her Health cannot independently diagnose medical conditions. If you experience concerning symptoms, consult a qualified healthcare professional.
125\. How does the chatbot work?
The Her Health chatbot uses predefined questions and responses to provide information about women's health. It identifies keywords or matching phrases in your question and returns an appropriate response.
126\. Can I ask the chatbot any health question?

127\. Why can't the chatbot answer my question?
The chatbot uses predefined responses. Your question may contain words or phrases that are not currently recognised. Try rephrasing your question using simpler words.
128\. Is my health information private?
Her Health is designed to support the privacy of users' health information. Refer to the application's privacy policy for information about how your data is collected, stored and used.
129\. Can I track my menstrual cycle?
Yes. Use the available menstrual health tracking functionality to record relevant cycle information and monitor patterns over time.
130\. Can I track my pregnancy?
Yes. The pregnancy tracking functionality allows you to monitor relevant pregnancy information and milestones.

The journal allows you to record personal experiences, symptoms, concerns and other health-related observations.
133\. Can I use Her Health if I am not pregnant?

━━━ 10\. IMPORTANT SAFETY RESPONSES ━━━
136\. What if I experience severe pain?
Severe pain should not be ignored. Please seek immediate medical assessment at your nearest healthcare facility.
137\. What if I experience heavy bleeding?
Heavy bleeding can be serious. Please seek emergency medical attention, especially if you feel dizzy, weak or faint.
138\. What if I experience difficulty breathing?
Difficulty breathing can be a medical emergency. Please seek emergency medical assistance immediately.
139\. What if I experience suicidal thoughts?
I'm sorry you're experiencing this. Your safety matters. Please contact emergency services or go to your nearest emergency department. If possible, stay with someone you trust.
